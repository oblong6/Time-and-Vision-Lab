## Presentations and discussion

### Talk 1: Individual Differences, Latent Structure and Time Perception (Liam Jordan)

* Slides: 
* Script (reference): 

**Summary:**
Liam presented a project investigating whether time perception operates via a unitary internal clock or multiple duration-selective channels, with a specific focus on socially meaningful timing using gaze duration. The core methodological approach uses individual differences and correlation structure across duration levels to infer latent architecture via factor analysis. The design includes a gaze-based ABABA 2AFC task and a matched non-social Gabor task, enabling both within-task and cross-task structural comparisons.

**Key feedback:**

* General engagement with the covariance-based approach and its novelty in the social timing domain
* Interest in the cross-task comparison as a strong test of shared vs separate timing architecture
* Discussion around interpretation of factor structure and robustness given five duration levels
* Clarification questions on task design, particularly ABABA structure and control of non-temporal cues

**Follow-ups:**

* Continue refining analysis plan, particularly factor extraction and interpretation
* Consider robustness checks for factor structure (e.g. alternative extraction methods)
* Further clarify how bandwidth estimates will be derived and interpreted
