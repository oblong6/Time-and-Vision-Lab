## Presentations and discussion

### Talk 1: Direct gaze bias, metacognitive awareness and autism (Princilla Agyemang)
- Slides: `./slides/Lab_Meeting_16_Mar_2026.pptx`
- Summary:
  - Princilla outlined a dissertation project examining direct gaze bias under uncertainty, and whether this bias reflects perceptual or decisional processes.
  - The project is grounded in a Bayesian account of perception, with the proposal that prior expectations exert a stronger influence under noisy or uncertain viewing conditions.
  - The planned study would use synthetic face stimuli with controlled gaze deviation and noise manipulation, together with confidence judgements to assess metacognitive awareness.
  - A key aim is to test whether autistic traits predict the magnitude of direct gaze bias and or reduced awareness of that bias.
- Key feedback:
  - Add references directly to claims in the slides.
  - Clarify the exact participant task, especially that the original paradigm is comparative rather than simply asking where a single face is looking.
  - Make the role of the standard face and comparison face explicit.
  - Be clearer on what the psychometric function is measuring and how perceptual versus decisional bias would be inferred.
  - Clarify the rationale for the autism component, specifically the distinction between weakened priors and difficulties accessing or reflecting on priors.
  - Check the original materials and implementation details more carefully, including Gorilla cloning issues and stimulus-generation workflow.
- Follow-ups:
  - Refine the slides and methods description to make the trial structure and logic explicit.
  - Revisit the original paper and paradigm to ensure the proposed task remains aligned with the source method.
  - Continue investigating Gorilla cloning issues and stimulus creation options.
